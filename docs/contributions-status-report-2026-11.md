## 1. Summary

Since the cooperative was founded on 22 July 2026, **K17,000** has been collected from members against an expected
**K32,900** for the five months July–November 2026. **K16,900** is outstanding, spread across 169 member-months,
giving a compliance rate of **49%**.

Three things need to be said plainly at the start:

- **Collection has been falling sharply month on month.** The monthly rate went 82% (July) → 82% (August) → 67%
  (September) → 42% (October). November is one day old and cannot yet be read either way.
- **A quarter of the register has never paid anything.** 16 of the 67 members on the register have no contribution on
  record for any month they owed. Seven of those have since left the cooperative, which means **K3,500 of the arrears
  are owed by people who are no longer with us** and are unlikely to be recovered.
- **K500 of the money received was collected from three members for months before they joined.** It is reported
  separately and is pending a committee decision (section 7). If it is credited forward rather than refunded, the
  amount actually collectable falls from K16,900 to **K16,400**.

The committee should treat the arrears as **two separate problems**: roughly K3,500 of dead debt from departed
members, and roughly K13,400 of live debt from members who are still on the register and can still be collected.

165 contributions were recorded. **160 of them fall inside the months the member was actually liable for**; the other

**The trend is downward and it is steep.** Comparing the first two months with the next two:

- Average collected per month fell from **K5,050** (July–August) to **K3,350** (September–October) — a **34% fall**.
- The number of members paying fell from 49 to 25 between July and October — a **49% fall** in the paying base.
- Only two payments have been received for November (CHISENGA ABEL and BANDA L, K100 each). **November must not be
  read as a collapse**; the month has barely started and it should be judged at the November meeting.

**Why the pattern is not simply "members stopped paying".** The timing of the entries shows the *recording* cycle
slipping as much as the paying cycle:

- July's contributions were received on **15 July**, in cash, each with a sequential receipt number
  (`RCT-2026-07-xxxxx`). This is what a properly collected month looks like.
- October's contributions were entered as a **mobile-money bulk import on 1 November** — that is, the money for
  October did not reach the ledger until the first day of the following month. One member (Mwansa Jacob) paid in cash
  on 28 October with a receipt.
- If that slippage repeats, November's contributions will land on 1 December, and the cooperative will spend the
  life of this report always one month behind its own ledger.

## 6. Recommendations

1. **Record the leaving month for the seven departed members** so their arrears can be closed off. All seven have
   paid nothing, so once their leaving months are on file the K3,500 will most likely fall away entirely. That alone
   would take the arrears from K16,900 to K13,400 and lift compliance from 49% to about 54%. This needs a "left at"
   field on the member record, which the platform does not currently have — the platform owner can add it and
   backfill once the chairman confirms the months.
2. **Make a personal approach to the nine active members who owe for every month** (K4,300 combined). These are not
   administrative cases; a phone call each will resolve whether they are refusing, unable, or simply unaware.
3. **Bring the recording date forward.** October's money was booked on 1 November. The committee should set a
   standing rule that a month's contributions are recorded by the **15th of the following month**, so that each
   month's ledger is closed before the next meeting.
4. **Capture the mobile-money receipt reference on every imported row.** The July cash entries carry sequential
   receipt numbers; the October import and the November entries show a blank receipt. The receipt number is the link
   between a row in the ledger and a line on the Airtel or MoMo statement, and without it reconciliation has to be
   done by hand.
5. **Decide what happens to the K500 received before joining** (section 7). It is either refunded to the three
   members or carried forward against their later months. The committee should make the call explicitly rather than
   letting it sit in the ledger.
6. **Put the arrears report on the monthly meeting agenda**, with a named follow-up against the largest balances.
   The **15 members owing K500 each account for K7,500 of the K16,900 — 44% of the money owed in a quarter of the
   names.** That is a short enough list to work through in one sitting.

## 7. The K500 received before joining

Three members were carried on the payroll list for months before their recorded joining dates:

| Service # | Name | Joined | Payments on record | Months paid inside their own window | Arrears |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 105734 | Mphande George | 6 Sep 2026 | Jul, Aug, Sep | 1 of 3 | K200 |
| 104916 | Chewe J | 7 Sep 2026 | Jul, Aug | 0 of 3 | K300 |
| 106000 | MUTALE JACK MWABA | 6 Aug 2026 | Jul, Aug | 1 of 4 | K300 |

106139 Mwansa Jacob is the one late joiner who is correct: he joined on 8 August and his payments are August,
September and October, all inside his own window.

This mattered because the arrears report used to count *how many* months a member had paid and subtract that from
*how many* months they owed. For the 63 founding members that is harmless, because their whole liability window is
July to November and every payment they made falls inside it. For a member who joined in September it is not: Mphande
George paid in July, August and September, so the old calculation gave 3 paid − 3 owed = 0 missed and showed him as
**100% compliant with K0 arrears** — when he has in fact not paid for October at all.

**What has changed.** The report now works out each member's liability month by month and counts the months they
owed that have no contribution for that month. Money received for a month outside that window is reported on its own
line rather than being quietly used to cancel an arrears month. The platform now also refuses a contribution for a
month before the member joined, in both the payroll import and the single-contribution form, and the import lists
such a row as invalid with the reason.

**What is still open.** The three members' joining dates carry placeholder phone numbers (`+260 97 100 0001` and
`+260 97 100 0007`) and blank ranks, so they may themselves be test data rather than the real dates from the
register. The K500 is held pending the chairman's confirmation.

- If it is **credited forward** against their later months, the arrears total falls from K16,900 to **K16,400**.
- If it is **refunded**, the arrears total stays at **K16,900** and the three members pay for their own months.

## 8. Notes on the figures

Two points so that the committee is not caught out by arithmetic that looks wrong but is not.

- **The monthly screen's "K6,000" is a target, not a liability.** It is calculated as 60 active members × K100 and
  assumes that figure for every month, ignoring the fact that four members joined in August and September and that
  seven who have since left were still on the register earlier in the year. Use the monthly screen for *cash in* and
  the arrears report for *what is owed*.
- **The K30,000 in the trend table and the K32,900 in the position table are both correct**, and the K2,900
  difference is fully explained: K3,500 is owed by the seven departed members who were still on the register during
  the earlier months, less K600 for the four late joiners who were not yet liable for all five months.
