**Reporting period:** 22 July 2026 (founding) to 1 November 2026

**Source:** BISWIC member platform (live ledger) and the contributions report export of 1 November 2026

---

## 1. Summary

Since the cooperative was founded on 22 July 2026, **K16,900** has been collected from members against an expected
**K32,900** for the five months July–November 2026. The balance of **K16,500** is outstanding, spread across 165
member-months, giving a compliance rate of **50%**.

Two things need to be said plainly at the start:

- **Collection has been falling sharply month on month.** The monthly rate went 82% (July) → 82% (August) → 67%
  (September) → 42% (October). November is one day old and cannot yet be read either way.
- **A third of the register has never paid anything.** 15 of the 67 members on the register have no contribution on
  record at all. Seven of those have since left the cooperative, which means **K3,500 of the arrears are owed by
  people who are no longer with us** and are unlikely to be recovered.

The committee should treat the K16,500 as **two separate problems**: roughly K3,500 of dead debt from departed
members, and roughly K13,000 of live debt from members who are still on the register and can still be collected.

**The trend is downward and it is steep.** Comparing the first two months with the next two:

- Average collected per month fell from **K5,050** (July–August) to **K3,350** (September–October) — a **34% fall**.
- The number of members paying fell from 49 to 25 between July and October — a **49% fall** in the paying base.
- Only the single payment received on 1 November is in the November figure. That is one member (CHISENGA ABEL,
  K100, mobile money). **November must not be read as a collapse**; the month has barely started and it should be
  judged at the November meeting.

**Why the pattern is not simply "members stopped paying".** The timing of the entries shows the *recording* cycle
slipping as much as the paying cycle:

- July's contributions were received on **15 July**, in cash, each with a sequential receipt number
  (`RCT-2026-07-xxxxx`). This is what a properly collected month looks like.
- October's contributions were entered as a **mobile-money bulk import on 1 November** — that is, the money for
  October did not reach the ledger until the first day of the following month.
- If that slippage repeats, November's contributions will land on 1 December, and the cooperative will spend the
  life of this report always one month behind its own ledger.

## 6. Recommendations

1. **Record the leaving month for the seven departed members** so their arrears can be closed off. All seven have
   paid nothing, so once their leaving months are on file the K3,500 will most likely fall away entirely. That alone
   would take the arrears from K16,500 to K13,000 and lift compliance from 50% to about 56%. This needs a "left at"
   field on the member record, which the platform does not currently have — the platform owner can add it and
   backfill once the chairman confirms the months.
2. **Make a personal approach to the eight active members who have never paid** (K4,000 combined). These are not
   administrative cases; a phone call each will resolve whether they are refusing, unable, or simply unaware.
3. **Bring the recording date forward.** October's money was booked on 1 November. The committee should set a
   standing rule that a month's contributions are recorded by the **15th of the following month**, so that each
   month's ledger is closed before the next meeting.
4. **Capture the mobile-money receipt reference on every imported row.** The July cash entries carry sequential
   receipt numbers; the October import and the November entry show a blank receipt. The receipt number is the link
   between a row in the ledger and a line on the Airtel or MoMo statement, and without it reconciliation has to be
   done by hand.
5. **Confirm the arrears rule for members who pay in advance.** Arrears are currently computed as *unpaid months ×
   K100*, which does not credit money paid above the current month. Four members have K500 in advance payments on the
   books, which is why "expected minus contributed" (K16,000) and the arrears figure (K16,500) differ by K500. The
   committee should decide whether an advance payment settles an earlier month. If it should, the arrears
   calculation needs to change; if it should not, the ledger needs a column to show the credit.
6. **Put the arrears report on the monthly meeting agenda**, with a named follow-up against the largest balances.
   The **16 members owing K400 to K500 each account for K7,900 of the K16,500 — 48% of the money owed in a quarter
   of the names.** That is a short enough list to work through in one sitting.
7. **Check the three late joiners against the founding register and decide how their July and August payments stand**
   (section 7). Until that is settled, the arrears total carries an uncertainty of K500, and the platform should stop
   recording contributions for months before a member joined.

## 7. A defect in the arrears calculation

105734 Mphande George shows as 3 of 3 months paid, K0 arrears, 100% compliance. The ledger tells a different story:

| Date paid | Month paid | Amount | Receipt |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 15 July 2026 | July 2026 | K100 | RCT-2026-07-105734 |
| 28 August 2026 | August 2026 | K100 | RCT-2026-08-105734 |
| 30 September 2026 | September 2026 | K100 | RCT-2026-09-105734 |

He joined the cooperative on **6 September 2026**, so he is liable for September, October and November. His last
payment was on 30 September. **He has not paid for October.**

The reason the report shows K0 is how arrears are calculated: the report counts *how many* months a member has paid
and subtracts that from *how many* months they owe. For Mphande George that is 3 − 3 = 0. It never checks *which*
months were paid. For the 63 founding members this makes no difference, because their whole liability window is July
to November and every payment they have made falls inside it. It only bites for members who joined after the
cooperative started.

Two other members are caught the same way, and the cause is in the recording process rather than in the
cooperative's money:

| Service # | Name | Joined | Payments on record | Months paid inside their own window |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 105734 | Mphande George | 6 Sep 2026 | Jul, Aug, Sep | 1 of 3 |
| 104916 | Chewe J | 7 Sep 2026 | Jul, Aug | 0 of 3 |
| 106000 | MUTALE JACK MWABA | 6 Aug 2026 | Jul, Aug | 1 of 4 |

All three were carried on the payroll for July, a month or two before their recorded joining dates. The payroll
import does not check a member's joining month before recording a contribution against their name, which the seed
code itself warns about: *"seeding them a January contribution would put them in arrears for months they were not
a member."* (106139 Mwansa Jacob is the one late joiner who is correct: he joined on 8 August and his payments are
August, September and October, all inside his own window.)

**What this is worth: K500 in total.**

- If the money they paid is **credited forward** against their later months, the arrears total stays at K16,500 and
  Mphande George genuinely owes nothing — but he is behind on October and November, and the report should say so
  rather than showing him as current.
- If those payments were **collected in error** and should be refunded, then each of the three must still pay for
  their own months, and the arrears total becomes **K17,000**.

One caution before the committee decides: the platform records for Mphande George and Chewe J carry placeholder
phone numbers (`+260 97 100 0001` and `+260 97 100 0007`) and blank ranks, so their joining dates may themselves be
seed data rather than the real dates from the register. Their entries should be checked against the founding
register before anything is written off or chased.

## 8. Notes on the figures

Two points so that the committee is not caught out by arithmetic that looks wrong but is not.

- **The monthly screen's "K6,000" is a target, not a liability.** It is calculated as 60 active members × K100 and
  assumes that figure for every month, ignoring the fact that four members joined in August and September and that
  seven who have since left were still on the register earlier in the year. Use the monthly screen for *cash in* and
  the arrears report for *what is owed*.
- **The K30,000 in the trend table and the K32,900 in the position table are both correct**, and the K2,900
  difference is fully explained: K3,500 is owed by the seven departed members who were still on the register during
  the earlier months, less K600 for the four late joiners who were not yet liable for all five months.
