## 1. PAYE and the civil service payslip

### The current PAYE bands (charge year 2026)

PAYE ("Pay As You Earn") is the income tax your employer withholds from your gross pay and pays to the Zambia Revenue Authority (ZRA) on your behalf. The tax is **progressive**: each slice of your income is taxed at its own rate, so you do **not** pay 37% on your whole salary just because you earn above the top band.

| Monthly taxable emoluments (K) | Rate | Annual equivalent (K) | Rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 – 5,100 | 0% (tax-free) | up to 61,200 | 0% |
| 5,100.01 – 7,100 | 20% | 61,201 – 85,200 | 20% |
| 7,100.01 – 9,200 | 30% | 85,201 – 110,400 | 30% |
| Above 9,200 | 37% | Above 110,400 | 37% |

Source: KPMG/UNDP *Zambia 2026 National Budget Highlights*, "Domestic Taxes" chapter — https://www.undp.org/sites/g/files/zskgke326/files/2025-10/undp_kpmg_zambia_2026_national_budget_highlights.pdf (checked 2 November 2026). Corroborated by PwC World Wide Tax Summaries, "Zambia – Individual – Taxes on personal income" (last reviewed 24 July 2026) — https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/zambia/individual/taxes-on-personal-income (checked 2 November 2026), and by PKF Zambia, *Zambia 2026 Budget Tax Highlights* — https://www.pkf-zambia.co.zm/media/x1gbzjrw/zambia-2026-budget-tax-highlights-by-pkf-zambia.pdf (checked 2 November 2026).

### What changed recently, and when

- **2026 budget (effective 1 January 2026): the bands did not change.** The tax-free threshold "remains the same in 2026 at ZMW 5,100 per month and ZMW 61,200 [per year]", and the monthly tax credit for differently abled persons "will remain the same at ZMW 600 per month".
  Source: KPMG/UNDP *Zambia 2026 National Budget Highlights* — https://www.undp.org/sites/g/files/zskgke326/files/2025-10/undp_kpmg_zambia_2026_national_budget_highlights.pdf (checked 2 November 2026).
- **All 2026 domestic tax changes took effect 1 January 2026.** Seven tax statutes were enacted, covering the Income Tax Act, Customs and Excise Duties Act, VAT Act, ZRA Act, Property Transfer Tax Act, the Mobile Money Transactions Levy Act, and a new Betting Levy Act.
  Source: MMLP Legal (Mulenga & Mulenga) — *Key Changes to Tax Legislation 2026* — https://mmlp.co.zm/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/Key_Changes_to_Tax_Legislation_2026_Legal_Alert_Full_1st_Issue_2026.pdf (checked 2 November 2026).
- **1 January 2025 — a change that raised many civil servants' PAYE.** The *Income Tax (Amendment) Act No. 22 of 2024* replaced section 37(1) of the Income Tax Act with a deduction that benefits the **employer**, not the employee. The employee relief for pension contributions was removed, so **NAPSA and NHIMA no longer reduce the amount your PAYE bands are applied to**.
  Source: Parliament of Zambia, *Income Tax (Amendment) Act No. 22 of 2024* (Act No. 22 of 2024), section 4, commencement 1 January 2025 — https://www.parliament.gov.zm/sites/default/files/documents/acts/Income%20Tax%20(Amendment)%20Act%20No.%2022%20of%202024.pdf (checked 2 November 2026); also Parliament bills record — https://www.parliament.gov.zm/node/12097 (checked 2 November 2026).
- **Sources conflict on the practical effect.** Several Zambian payroll consultancies (e.g. M&J Consultants, uKhani) still deduct NAPSA *before* computing PAYE, which understates PAYE. Others (ZamCalc, payrollmaster.africa, countrytaxcalc) correctly state PAYE is charged on the **full gross** and that NAPSA/NHIMA are deducted alongside, not before. **The statutory position (Income Tax (Amendment) Act No. 22 of 2024, effective 1 January 2025) supports the latter.** If your payslip appears to reduce PAYE by your NAPSA deduction, query it with the payroll office and ZRA.
  Sources (both sides, checked 2 November 2026): https://mjconsultants.co.zm/insights/test-your-2026-zambia-payroll-software-in-five-minutes/ (deducts NAPSA first) vs https://zamcalc.com/blog/paye-tax-bands-zambia-2026 and https://payrollmaster.africa/for-accountants/zambia/ (PAYE on full gross).

### Other things a civil servant should know about PAYE

- **Allowances are taxable.** Housing, transport, education, domestic utility (electricity/telephone/water), commuted car and settling-in allowances are all taxable under PAYE. If your employer pays your rent, electricity or school fees directly, that amount is added to your emoluments and taxed.
  Source: Sage *Zambia Tax Summary 2026* — https://za-kb.sage.com/portal/app/portlets/results/viewdocument.jsp?solutionid=260119074758290 (checked 2 November 2026).
- **A second job is taxed at the top rate.** Any second or subsequent employment is treated as part-time and taxed at the highest marginal rate of 37% with no tax credits.
  Source: PaySpace, *Zambia Payroll Tax Guide 2026* — https://www.payspace.com/media/ZM-PAYROLL-SOLUTION-TAX-GUIDE-SUMMARY-2026.pdf (checked 2 November 2026).
- **Tax credit for differently abled persons: K600 per month.**
  Source: KPMG/UNDP *Zambia 2026 National Budget Highlights* — https://www.undp.org/sites/g/files/zskgke326/files/2025-10/undp_kpmg_zambia_2026_national_budget_highlights.pdf (checked 2 November 2026).
- **If you are over-taxed, you can ask for a refund.** PAYE is refundable where it has been overcharged in a charge year; employers must keep payroll records for 6 years after the charge year.
  Source: Sage *Zambia Tax Summary 2026* — https://za-kb.sage.com/portal/app/portlets/results/viewdocument.jsp?solutionid=260119074758290 (checked 2 November 2026); ZRA Taxation Practice Note No. 1 of 2025 (parliament/practice-note copy) — https://www.scribd.com/document/841159247/Taxation-Practice-Notes-No1-2025-v4 (checked 2 November 2026). *The Practice Note copy here is a secondary re-upload; the ZRA original was not reachable.*
- **Arrears (back pay) are normally taxed in the months they were earned**, under section 21(4), which stops a single back-pay lump being pushed into the 37% band.
  Source: ZamCalc *Zambia Salary Arrears Calculator 2026* — https://zamcalc.com/calculators/arrears (checked 2 November 2026). *Secondary source.*
- **One-off payments are taxed in the month you receive them.** A bonus is taxed in the month it is paid, which can push part of it into the 37% band.
  Source: Sage *Zambia Tax Summary 2026* — https://za-kb.sage.com/portal/app/portlets/results/viewdocument.jsp?solutionid=260119074758290 (checked 2 November 2026).

### The National Housing Fund

The National Housing Authority (NHA), established under the National Housing Authority Act (Chapter 195, 1971), was mandated "to provide, manage and control housing accommodation for public officers and employees of the Government and of prescribed organisations" and "to establish a national housing revolving fund to provide finance for housing throughout the Republic". NHA loans to individuals for acquiring land and constructing approved houses bear interest at rates approved by the Minister responsible for finance, with loan tenures of up to 30 years for individual housing loans.
Source: *National Housing Authority Act, 1971 (Chapter 195)* — https://media.zambialii.org/media/legislation/19748/source_file/dde494245e06f623/1971-16.pdf (checked 2 November 2026).

## 2. NAPSA and pensions

### What your 5% buys

NAPSA is Zambia's mandatory funded pension scheme for the formal sector. Your employer deducts **5% of your gross monthly earnings** and adds a matching **5% of its own** — 10% of your pay in total goes into your pension account each month. The employer's 5% is *extra*: it does not reduce your take-home pay.

|---|---|---|

| 2026 insurable earnings ceiling | K37,236 per month | https://communityhub.sage.com/za/sage-pastel-payroll/f/announcements/262250/msi-release-angola-ghana-kenya-malawi-and-zambia-released-19-january-2026 (checked 2 November 2026) |

### The National Pension Scheme Act, 2026 — what it changes

**This is the big 2026 story, and it is real.** The *National Pension Scheme Act No. 72 of 2026* repeals and replaces the 1996 Act. It was assented to on **4 June 2026** and published on 12 June 2026.

| Change | Detail |
|---|---|
| Minimum monthly pension | Raised from **K1,861 to K2,327** in 2026; the benchmark moved from **20% to 25% of National Average Earnings**. **17,806 pensioners** benefited; total monthly payments rose from **K31.8 million (June 2026) to K39.6 million (July 2026)**. |
| Income replacement rate | Improved from **40% to 45%**, without raising the contribution rate |
| 30% near-retirement lump sum | A member may draw **30% of indexed monthly contributions plus interest** any time from **three months before retirement** until the day before retiring |
| 20% pre-retirement lump sum | **Retained** (see below) |
| Interest-free advance payments | New facility launched **8 August 2026**; NAPSA disbursed **K9,137,772.92 to 565 pensioners within three days**; **34,000** pensioners were eligible as at 1 August 2026 |
| Retirement age | **Unchanged** — normal 60, early 55, late 65 |
| Contribution rate | **Unchanged** at 10%, split 5%/5% |
| Minimum pension floor after early withdrawals | 80% of the minimum pension for members who took the 20%; **70%** for members who took both the 20% and the 30% |
| Employer enforcement | Employer must register within **one month** of hiring its first employee; late contributions attract a **10% penalty per month**; inspectors may enter premises without prior notice on reasonable grounds |
| Governance | Board re-constituted with greater private-sector representation and **mandatory inclusion of an investment expert and an actuary** |
| New powers | Sub-schemes (including voluntary savings platforms for the diaspora), joint ventures, partnerships and co-investment |

Sources: Parliament of Zambia, *The National Pension Scheme Act, 2026* (Act No. 72 of 2026) — https://www.parliament.gov.zm/sites/default/files/documents/acts/Act%20No.%2072,%20The%20National%20Pension%20Scheme%20Act%20(1).pdf (checked 2 November 2026); Parliament Acts register — https://www.parliament.gov.zm/acts-of-parliament (checked 2 November 2026); NAPSA, *FAQs: National Pension Scheme Act, 2026* — https://www.napsa.co.zm/download/publication/NAPSA_Pension_reforms_FAQs_V02 (checked 2 November 2026); NAPSA press release, *NAPSA Implements Pension Uplift, Benefiting Over 17,800 Pensioners Nationwide*, 7 August 2026 — https://napsa.co.zm/news/details?id=dt91c3bb-5416-4a53-b521-73923e0ccf112 (checked 2 November 2026); NAPSA press release, *NAPSA Disburses K9.1 Million in Interest-Free Advances to 565 Pensioners*, 11 August 2026 — https://www.napsa.co.zm/news/details?id=dt91c3tb-5416-4a53-b521-73923e0ccf112 (checked 2 November 2026).

**Sources conflict on when the Act came into force.** The Act itself says it "shall come into operation on the date appointed by the Minister" by statutory instrument. Some commentary written before August 2026 states the Act was "enacted but not in force" and that the 30% lump sum was not yet claimable. NAPSA's own press release of 7 August 2026 states that the reforms were brought into effect by "the issuance of **Statutory Instrument No. 62 of 2026**" — and NAPSA has already paid the uplift and the 30% and interest-free advances. **Treat the Act as in force**, but confirm the current position on the 30% lump sum with NAPSA directly, because the SI text itself was not reachable.

**Important:** the 30% is **not** "20% plus another 30%". If you already took the 20%, the near-retirement access is 10%, giving 30% total lump-sum access; if you have not taken the 20%, near-retirement access may be up to 30%. Either way, the balance of your retirement pension is recalculated on your *residual* contributions, and your minimum-pension floor drops (to 80% or 70%).

### How your pension is calculated

NAPSA's official benefits guide gives these formulas:

| Benefit | Formula |
|---|---|
| Lump sum (all lump sums) | Total employee contributions + total employer contributions + indexation (compensation for wage inflation) + interest |
| Normal retirement pension | Average Indexed Monthly Earnings × (0.001111 × M), where M = months of pensionable employment |
| Early retirement pension | Normal retirement pension − (0.005 × Normal retirement pension × M), where M = months between early retirement and normal retirement age |
| Invalidity pension | (0.005 × Average Indexed Monthly Earnings × number of years lost from work due to invalidity) + the greater of the normal retirement pension or the minimum pension |
| Minimum monthly pension | 25% of National Average Earnings for the year (was 20% before the 2026 Act) |

Source: NAPSA, *Benefits Guide* — https://www.napsa.co.zm/download/publication/NAPSA_Benefits_Brochure_2024 (checked 2 November 2026); National Pension Scheme Act No. 72 of 2026, sections 30 and 33 — https://www.parliament.gov.zm/sites/default/files/documents/acts/Act%20No.%2072,%20The%20National%20Pension%20Scheme%20Act%20(1).pdf (checked 2 November 2026).

**"Average monthly earnings"** is calculated for each member at the time of the claim by dividing the aggregate of pensionable earnings by the number of months of pensionable employment, and the level of pensionable earnings is adjusted annually by an index based on national average earnings (Zambia Statistics Agency data).
Source: National Pension Scheme Act No. 72 of 2026, section 30(3) — https://www.parliament.gov.zm/sites/default/files/documents/acts/Act%20No.%2072,%20The%20National%20Pension%20Scheme%20Act%20(1).pdf (checked 2 November 2026).

### What a civil servant household should know

- **Do not touch your pension early without a number.** Taking 20% or 30% now permanently reduces the pension you receive at 60, and reduces the minimum-pension floor you can fall back on. Work out the monthly pension first, then the lump sum.
- **Check that you have 180 months of contributions.** If you have changed jobs often, ask NAPSA for your contribution statement — missing months can cost you the right to a pension at all.
- **Your 5% is an investment, not a tax.** It is the only retirement "income" most civil servants will ever have, and the 2026 Act improved what it buys without asking you to pay more.
- **Voluntary top-ups exist.** The new Act keeps voluntary contributions for informal-sector workers and those not in formal employment, and creates sub-schemes including voluntary savings platforms for Zambians abroad.
  Source: NAPSA *FAQs: National Pension Scheme Act, 2026* — https://www.napsa.co.zm/download/publication/NAPSA_Pension_reforms_FAQs_V02 (checked 2 November 2026).

### The mobile money levy — what changed on 1 January 2026

This is a **fixed fee per transaction band, not a percentage**. It is charged on **person-to-person mobile transfers**, and **the sender pays it** — not the person receiving the money. It sits **on top of** the operator's own fee, so your total cost is operator fee **plus** levy. Cash withdrawals and merchant payments are reported as carrying **no levy**.

**New levy schedule (in force 1 January 2026):**

| Transaction amount (ZMW) | Levy from 1 Jan 2026 (ZMW) | Previous levy from 1 Jan 2025 (ZMW) |
|---|---|---|
| From 0 to 150 | 0.32 | 0.16 |
| Above 150 to 300 | 0.40 | 0.20 |
| Above 300 to 500 | 0.80 | 0.40 |
| Above 500 to 1,000 | 2.00 | 1.00 |
| Above 1,000 to 3,000 | 4.00 | 1.60 |
| Above 3,000 to 5,000 | 7.50 | 2.00 |
| Above 5,000 to 10,000 | 8.00 | 3.00 |
| Above 10,000 | 10.00 | 3.60 |

Source (2026 schedule): Parliament of Zambia, *Mobile Money Transaction Levy (Amendment) Act, 2025* (Act No. 22 of 2025, assented 23 December 2025, in operation 1 January 2026), new Schedule — https://www.parliament.gov.zm/sites/default/files/documents/acts/Act%20No.%2022%20of%202025%20The%20Mobile%20Money%20Transaction%20Levy%20(Amendment)..pdf (checked 2 November 2026). Commencement confirmed by ZambiaLII — https://zambialii.org/akn/zm/act/2025/22/eng@2025-12-30 (checked 2 November 2026).
Source (2025 schedule, for comparison): Parliament of Zambia, *Mobile Money Transaction Levy Act, 2024* (Act No. 25 of 2024, in operation 1 January 2025) — https://www.parliament.gov.zm/sites/default/files/documents/acts/Act%20No.%2025%20of%202024,%20The%20Mobile%20Money%20Transaction%20Levy.pdf (checked 2 November 2026).
Also: PwC, *Zambia – Corporate – Other taxes* — https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/zambia/corporate/other-taxes (checked 2 November 2026); MMLP, *Key Changes to Tax Legislation 2026* — https://mmlp.co.zm/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/Key_Changes_to_Tax_Legislation_2026_Legal_Alert_Full_1st_Issue_2026.pdf (checked 2 November 2026).

**In plain English: the 1 January 2026 change made the levy roughly double on most bands, and up to 2.8× on the top band.** The biggest jumps are on transfers above K1,000 — on a K5,000 transfer the levy rose from K2.00 to K7.50.

**Sources conflict on two details:**
1. **The start date.** The Act's own commencement clause says 1 January 2026 (confirmed by ZambiaLII), but one commentary says the revised bands "took effect on 4 January 2026". Treat **1 January 2026** as correct.
   Sources in conflict (checked 2 November 2026): https://zambialii.org/akn/zm/act/2025/22/eng@2025-12-30 (1 January 2026) vs https://zambianeconomist.com/zambia-mobile-money-levy-2026/ (4 January 2026).
2. **Whether the levy applies to cash withdrawals.** The dominant reading across several sources is that it applies only to person-to-person transfers and not to withdrawals or bank transfers. One source presents it as applying generally to transfers. **The levy-only-on-transfers reading is the better supported one**, but the practical advice is the same either way: a cash-out costs you the operator's withdrawal fee, and there is no levy on top of it.
   Sources (checked 2 November 2026): https://metricsuite.tools/zambia-mobile-money-fee-calculator/ and https://momocalc.com/zambia (transfers only) vs https://zambianeconomist.com/zambia-mobile-money-levy-2026/ (general framing).

**Levy administration moved to ZRA.** Under the 2024 Act, ZRA became the primary authority for the levy; mobile money service providers must remit collected levies **directly to ZRA within ten days after the end of each month**, and BoZ must hand over previously collected levy records to ZRA.
Source: PwC, *Zambia – Corporate – Other taxes* — https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/zambia/corporate/other-taxes (checked 2 November 2026).

### Interoperability — you are no longer locked to one wallet

Zambia's **National Financial Switch (NFS)** links ATMs, point-of-sale and mobile money onto one platform. It was officially launched on **19 June 2019**, and **instant payments went live in April 2020**, enabling interoperability among banks, non-banks, fintechs and mobile money service providers — including wallet-to-wallet and wallet-to-bank transfers.
Source: Bank of Zambia, *National Payment System Biennial Report on the Oversight of Financial Market Infrastructures*, June 2026 — https://www.boz.zm/sites/default/files/2026-06/National%20Payment%20System%20Biennial%20Report%20on%20the%20Oversight%20of%20Financial%20Market%20Infrastructures.pdf (checked 2 November 2026).

The 2026 Act of Parliament adds that a payment service provider "shall not provide for contract terms and conditions that impose barriers for a consumer to switch from that payment service provider or payment service to another", and that a provider "shall not engage in coercive behaviour that compels a consumer to make decisions that are not in the consumer's best interests".

### Fraud: the three risks that catch Zambians

**1. SIM swap.** Someone fraudulently takes over your phone number so your mobile money PIN and OTP resets land with them. ZICTA's SIM registration rules (SI 10 of 2026) now require an ID document, a facial photo and validation against the national identity management system before a SIM is activated, cap individuals at **five SIMs**, and require parents to register a child's SIM until the child turns 18.
Source: Shufti, *KYC Zambia: NRC Checks, PACRA KYB and AML Screening* — https://shuftipro.com/supported-countries/zambia/ (checked 2 November 2026). *Secondary source summarising SI 10 of 2026 — the SI itself was not reachable.*

**2. Phone theft / PIN compromise.** If you lose your phone or suspect someone knows your PIN, MTN's terms require you to **immediately** contact MoMo customer service. Until they receive your notice, they remain authorised to process requests made with your PIN — so speed matters. MTN also states it is "not liable for any loss or damage resulting from the disclosure of your PIN".
Source: MTN Zambia, *MTN Mobile Money Terms & Conditions*, "Loss of Mobile Phone and Unauthorized Transactions" and "Your Equipment and Responsibilities" — https://www.mtn.zm/terms-conditions/ (checked 2 November 2026).

**3. Fake or dishonest agents.** The Bank of Zambia's National Payment Systems Vision and Strategy 2023–2027 commits to building "a register for persons/agents involved in fraud or dishonest activities — a centralised industry accessible database/registry … with a view for blacklisting persons and/or agents that are non-compliant with relevant laws and regulations or are involved in fraudulent or dishonest activities". In practice, use agents the operator has appointed, ask for the fee before the transaction, and never hand over your PIN.
Source: Bank of Zambia, *National Payment Systems Vision and Strategy 2023–2027* — https://www.boz.zm/sites/default/files/2026-01/National_Payment_Systems_Vision_Strategy_2023_2027.pdf (checked 2 November 2026).

**A national fraud-awareness campaign is running.** The Global System for Mobile Association of Zambia (GSMAZ), with the Bank of Zambia and other stakeholders, has launched a nationwide campaign to raise public awareness of mobile-related fraud.
Source: Bank of Zambia, *Zambanker*, September 2025 edition — https://www.boz.zm/sites/default/files/2026-03/Q3%20Edition%20-%20Zambanker%202025.pdf (checked 2 November 2026).

### What a Zambian civil servant household should know

- **The person who sends pays the levy.** If your child or a relative sends you money, the levy is their cost, not yours. But if *you* send, budget for the operator fee **plus** the levy.
- **Sending to a number that is not registered on that network costs several times more.** Make sure the recipient has an account before you send.
- **Cross-network transfers cost roughly three times a same-network transfer.** Where both parties are on the same network, that is cheaper.
- **Cash-out is the expensive part.** Receiving is free; getting cash into your hand is what costs you.
- **Upgrade to Tier 2 before you need it.** Tier 1 caps you at K20,000 a day; Tier 2 raises that to K100,000, and requires your NRC, proof of residence and an employer's referral — a civil servant can supply all three.
- **Never give your PIN to anyone**, including an agent. If money leaves your wallet without your instruction, call your operator immediately and then complain to the Bank of Zambia.

### What to do when a bank refuses you a loan

**First, understand why.** The Bank of Zambia has found that "the negative listing has been used to deny credit" by some lenders, and that this "is not the intention of the credit reporting system". A negative entry should lead to a **higher price or tighter conditions**, not an automatic refusal.

### What a Zambian civil servant household should know about banking

- **Ask for the "all-in" cost before you sign**: the interest rate, the arrangement/arrangement fee, the insurance premium, and every monthly fee. The published rate is not the cost of the loan.
- **A salary-backed account is your best asset.** Banks price salary-backed loans lowest because your pay is visible. Keep your salary account open and in good standing even after you retire from a particular bank.
- **Save first, borrow second.** A 6-month deposit rate around 7.4% and savings rates up to 22% at some banks (March 2026 averages) beat most things you will be offered on a loan.
- **If refused, ask for the reason in writing.** "Credit report" is a starting point for a challenge, not the end of the conversation.

### How credit scoring works in Zambia

**Zambia has one licensed credit reference agency.** The *Credit Reporting Act No. 8 of 2018* provides for the regulation of credit reporting agencies by the Bank of Zambia, the licensing of credit reporting agencies, and the establishment of a **Credit Registry** — a database of credit information held by the Bank. The Bank of Zambia has licensed **Credit Reference Bureau Africa Limited (CRB)** to provide credit reference services in Zambia. The Act also covers the collection, accuracy, use, security, access and **correction** of credit information, and prohibits any person who is not duly licensed from carrying on credit reference services.
Source: Corpus, *Credit Referencing: the permutations of the Court of Appeal decision in Melissa Supermarket v Stanbic Bank Zambia Ltd [2022] ZMCA 17* — https://www.corpus.co.zm/credit-referencing-the-permutations-of-the-court-of-appeal-decision-in-the-case-of-melissa-supermarket-and-another-vs-stanbic-bank-zambia-limited-2022-zmca-17-the-melissa-decision/ (checked 2 November 2026). *Secondary legal commentary; the Act text was not reachable.*

**Using a credit bureau is compulsory for lenders.** Under the *Banking and Financial Services (Provision of Credit Information and Utilisation of Credit Reporting Agencies) Directive, 2020*, a financial service provider must (i) submit credit information on every borrower to a credit reporting agency, and (ii) **use** a credit reporting agency's services when assessing a customer who has applied for credit **above ZMW 500** (or equivalent). Breach attracts an **administrative penalty of up to 200,000 fee units** plus supervisory action.
Source: Bank of Zambia, CB Circular No. 08 of 2020 — https://www.boz.zm/sites/default/files/2026-01/CBCIRCULARNO.08OF2020BOZDIRECTIVE2020_0.pdf (checked 2 November 2026).

**What actually drives your score** — it is not a black box number you were born with. It is your **record**: whether you have borrowed before and paid on time, whether you are in arrears now, how many loans you have open, and whether your records are even correct.
Source: TransUnion Africa, *Zambia* — https://www.transunionafrica.com/zambia (checked 2 November 2026).

**Does the credit bureau cover civil servants? Yes — and that is exactly the point.** The Bank of Zambia's 2026 public notice states that its financial stability assessments found "increasing incidences of some public workers obtaining credit not based on the payroll (off-payroll credit)", and that some lending institutions had "inconsistent use of the Credit Reference Bureau (CRB), or disregard of data from the Bureau altogether". That is a statement that **CRB data on public workers exists and is being used**. The Bank has directed lenders to use it consistently.
Source: https://www.boz.zm/news-detail/57b36084-09cf-40a3-a162-4ca14e0d14da (checked 2 November 2026).

**The upside for a civil servant:** because you have a stable, verifiable salary, you can build a credit history from scratch. A salary-backed loan paid on time for two years is a stronger asset than any inheritance.

### The true cost of borrowing, in plain English

**Example 1 — a bank personal loan.** You borrow **K100,000** over **12 months** at **30% per annum**, and the bank deducts the interest from the first payment:

| | Amount |
|---|---|
| Amount received | K100,000 |
| Interest charged over 12 months (30% of K100,000) | K30,000 |
| **Total you repay** | **K130,000** |
| Monthly repayment | K10,833 |
| If it were interest-free, the monthly repayment would be | K8,333 |
| **Extra you pay** | **K2,500 a month — K30,000 in total** |

**Example 2 — the informal lender.** A "10% a month" flat charge on a **K5,000** loan over **4 weeks**:

| | Amount |
|---|---|
| Interest charged flat on the original K5,000 | K500 |
| You repay | K5,500 |
| Flat rate for the month | 10% |
| **If it compounded monthly for 12 months, the rate would be** | **214% a year** |

**The rule to teach:** always ask for the **total amount you will repay** and **how many months** it takes. Divide and compare. If a lender will not give you the total, walk away.
Source for the flat-rate arithmetic and the "10% per week" informal benchmark: ZamCalc, *Microloan Calculator Zambia 2026* — https://zamcalc.com/calculators/microloan (checked 2 November 2026). *Secondary source; the arithmetic is calculated from the published rate.*

### The danger of borrowing to repay borrowing

This is a real, named and monitored problem in Zambia — and it is specifically a problem among public workers.

**What the Bank of Zambia found:** "Some credit is often beyond permissible limits, often through misrepresentation, non-disclosure of existing obligations, and **multiple borrowing across institutions**." The Bank's response: compliance with **debt-service-ratio (DSR)** limits and affordability standards, and consistent use of CRB data, "is mandatory". All regulated lenders are directed "to strictly adhere to debt-service-ratio limits and adopt responsible lending practices", and payroll administrators are told to strengthen internal payroll controls and recovery mechanisms and to take disciplinary action where abuse of payroll-based deduction arrangements is found.
Source: Bank of Zambia public notice — https://www.boz.zm/news-detail/57b36084-09cf-40a3-a162-4ca14e0d14da (checked 2 November 2026).

**What it looks like in practice:** a household with a salary-backed loan at the bank, a second loan on a "quick cash" app, and a third from a savings group. When one payment date slips, the household borrows from the next lender to make the first one. This is called **debt cycling**, and it is how a K50,000 problem becomes a K500,000 problem.

**The five warning signs:**
1. You are borrowing from a new lender because a payment is due from an old one.
2. You are using one credit card or mobile-money overdraft to pay another.
3. You do not know the total balance of your borrowing, only that you are behind.
4. You are paying a fee to *access* a loan (a "consultation fee", a "processing fee" paid before disbursement).
5. The lender does not check the credit bureau, or asks you not to disclose other loans.

**What to do instead:** list every loan with lender, balance, rate and payment date on one page. If the total monthly repayment exceeds what you can pay if you lose one income stream, stop taking new credit and speak to the lender **before** you miss a payment. Repaying an existing loan early, or consolidating into one longer, cheaper loan, is nearly always better than adding a third.

### What a Zambian civil servant household should know about borrowing

- **Your salary is your strongest financial asset — do not attach it to more than one expensive loan.** Off-payroll borrowing is what the Bank of Zambia is actually worried about.
- **Never borrow from a lender who does not check the credit bureau.** If they do not check, they do not know what you owe, and they will lend to you again.
- **A SACCO loan against your own savings is usually the cheapest money you can borrow.** Your savings sit as security, and you are effectively paying yourself the difference.
- **Anything above 48% per year from a money-lender is unlawful.** Demand the written contract.
- **Compare on total repayable, not on the monthly instalment.** A small monthly instalment on a long term is how people end up paying double.

## 6. Land and housing

### First, the legal fact that changes everything

**In Zambia you never own land outright.** All land is held from the State on a **renewable 99-year lease** granted by the Lands Commission. "Land allocation does not confer ownership." When you buy land, you are buying the **leasehold**, and the State must consent to the transfer.
Sources: US Department of State, *2026 Investment Climate Statements: Zambia* — https://www.state.gov/reports/2026-investment-climate-statements/zambia (checked 2 November 2026); National Housing Authority Act (Cap. 195), sections on allocation — https://media.zambialii.org/media/legislation/19748/source_file/dde494245e06f623/1971-16.pdf (checked 2 November 2026).

**There is also no Zambian "National Housing Corporation".** The Zambian body is the **National Housing Authority (NHA)**, established under the National Housing Authority Act, 1971 (Cap. 195). "NHC" is the Tanzanian and Kenyan name — a common trap when searching online. See the "Not verified" section.

### The cost of buying land: the fees you actually pay

> **Correction to the brief — who registers land in Zambia.** The brief asked for "PACRA property registration fees". **PACRA (the Patents and Companies Registration Agency) does not register land.** Its mandate is business registration and intellectual property — trademarks, patents, copyright, industrial designs, business names, incorporation of companies, and the **movable-property collateral registry**. Land is registered by the **Lands and Deeds Registry** under the **Lands and Deeds Registry Act (Cap 185)**, run by the **Ministry of Lands and Natural Resources**, while the **Commissioner of Lands** (Lands Commission) grants the State's consent to a transfer. Every land fee in this section therefore comes from the Lands and Deeds Registry, the Ministry of Lands, or ZRA — **not** from PACRA.

**A. Property Transfer Tax (PTT) — 5%**
Rate of tax on the transfer of land: **5%** (10% for a mining right), charged on **5% of the contract sum or 5% of the value assessed by ZRA**, whichever is higher. ZRA states clearly: **"The PTT on land, shares and mining rights is paid by the seller."**
Source: ZRA, *Property Tax* — https://www.zra.org.zm/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/Property-Tax.pdf (checked 2 November 2026); *Property Transfer Tax Act, Cap 340* via Knight Frank guide — https://content.knightfrank.com/research/2422/documents/en/a-guide-to-buying-property-in-zambia-2022-8906.pdf (checked 2 November 2026).
**Sources conflict on who bears it.** The Knight Frank cost table lists "Property Transfer Tax 5% — **Buyer**". ZRA is the administering authority and states the seller pays it. **In practice it is a negotiable commercial point** — it is usually deducted from the price, which is what the buyer ultimately funds. Tell learners: *ask who is paying it, because it changes the price you agreed.*

### What a Zambian civil servant household should know about land and housing

- **Buy the leasehold, get the title deed.** Nothing is yours until the deed is issued in your name by the Lands and Deeds Registry. Never pay the balance before that.
- **Ground rent is annual and it kills deals.** If it has not been paid, the Commissioner of Lands will not consent to the transfer. Ask for the ground rent receipt.
- **Budget the fees, not just the price.** PTT is 5% (whoever pays it), registration is either 1% capped at K15,000 or 2%, consent is K333.60, and valuation on anything over K500,000. That is why a "cheap" plot can cost much more than the asking price by the time you hold the deed.
- **Buy near the road and near services.** Plots that gain power and water connections after you buy appreciate 40–60% within 6–12 months in the satellite areas — and plots that never get them do not.
- **Do the arithmetic before you commit:** the rate on a K550,000 plot at 30% over 20 years, against a salary-backed loan at the same rate on land you could have bought in Chongwe for K30,000, is the difference between owning a house and owning a bill.
- **Renting first is not failure.** The NHA's rent-to-own and the ZUGGAU/Collins Mutual scheme of 40,000 civil-servant units exist precisely because most Zambians cannot buy and build in one step.
